Help that lives where the question comes up

Six patterns, from inline to full page. Each one answers a single question: what is this, what happens next, or why is it like this. All of them are written once, in the repository, and reviewed like code.

1 · Term tip: “what is this?”
Rollover · 6.0 h ?
Rollover: retainer hours you didn’t use that carry into later months. These came from August and expire Nov 30. More about rollover

For any word a new user may not know. Opens on click or focus, never only on hover, so it works on touch and with a keyboard. The text comes from one glossary, so “Approved” means the same thing on every screen.

2 · Status with its next step
ReviewedWaiting on Acme to approve
DisputedFix with a correction. The issued invoice isn’t edited.
ApprovedBy Dana Okafor, Oct 8 · final

A badge never stands alone. It always says who or what it’s waiting on. Every status passes through statusLabel, which now returns a label and a next-step line. A test fails if any status is missing either.

3 · Consequence before action
Issue invoice #1042 for $8,000?
  • Locks 38.5 h and their rates
  • Emails Dana Okafor the invoice and a payment link
  • Paying it approves the hours, unless Acme disputes any
CancelIssue and send

For anything that can’t be undone: issue, send, void, approve, end an agreement. The dialog lists what will happen, and the button names the action (“Issue and send”), never “OK”. The server sends this list with the action’s URL, so the dialog can’t describe something different from what happens.

4 · Empty state that teaches
No partner hours to review
When a partner on a shared project submits a month and gives you review access, it shows up here. Invite one from the project’s page.
How shared projects work

An empty list says what would appear there, why it would, and the one step that makes it happen. No mascot artwork.

5 · Help panel and guide
Help · Hours to approve? or ⌘/
▸ What does approving a month do?
▸ What if I disagree with one entry?
▸ Why are two companies’ hours here?

Help opens beside the page and shows questions about that page first, with search across everything. The same articles make up a full guide at /help, which also shows when you’re signed out. Operators, clients and partners each see only the articles written for them.

6 · First-run checklist
Set up your workspace · 2 of 4
✓ Add your first client
✓ Create an agreement
3 · Connect a way to get paid (Stripe or Square)
4 · Invite a client contact to their portal

The checklist depends on your role: an operator gets one, a client contact gets one, and an invited partner gets a three-step one. Each step is checked off because the thing actually exists, not because a tour was clicked through. You can dismiss it, and it stays dismissed.

Where it lives

docs/help/*.md: one Markdown file per article. Its front matter names the audience (operator, client, partner), the routes it belongs to, and the glossary terms it defines. It’s built into the app, so help ships in the same commit as the feature it explains.

Kept honest by tests

Tests fail when a route has no help article, a status has no next-step line, a glossary term is defined twice, or an article links to a heading that doesn’t exist. Numbers in examples come from the same calculation classes the app uses, never typed by hand.

Words, once

Draft · Submitted · Reviewed · Invoiced · Approved · Disputed · Retainer · Rollover · Overage · Shared project · Partner. One definition each, used in the app, in emails, in PDFs and over MCP.
