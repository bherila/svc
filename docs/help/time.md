Time is stored in whole minutes and shown in the style you choose in the account menu, as 2.5 h or 2:30. Type it any way you like: 2:30, 2.5, 2h30m or 150m all mean the same.

## How time moves through review

Time starts as a **Draft**. Once handed in it is **Submitted**, then **Reviewed** by the business that will invoice it. When the client agrees it is **Approved**, and when it lands on an issued invoice it is **Invoiced**.

Nobody approves their own time. Your own hours go straight onto the client's draft invoice when it is made, with no approval in between, and the client approves them afterwards: in their portal, or by paying the invoice. Someone else's hours, such as a subcontractor's, can be approved by an owner, an admin or a project manager.

## What Draft means

Draft time is yours alone. It is not counted anywhere until you send it on.

## What Submitted means

Submitted time is waiting for the business, or a reviewer the business named, to look at it. You can no longer change it until you withdraw it. See [submitting hours and expenses](help:submitting-hours) for how.

## What Reviewed means

Reviewed time has been checked and is waiting for the client to approve it.

## What Approved means

Approved time is ready to bill and will go on the next invoice. For your own hours, being pulled onto a draft invoice is what makes them ready; the approver is then left blank because no person approved them. The client's own approval is separate and does not change what is billed.

## What Disputed means

Disputed time is questioned by the client. It stays out of billing until the question is settled: no draft invoice takes it, and if it was already on a draft, the draft is rebuilt without it and lists it under *Still deferred* with a **Disputed** tag. The time sheet marks it **Disputed** and shows what the client wrote; **Disputed** at the top of the sheet shows only those entries.

Time on an issued invoice is different: the charge stands until you correct the invoice. Disputing it doesn't take it off.

To settle a dispute, choose **Respond to dispute** on the entry and write a reply. The client reads the reply exactly as you write it. Then choose one of:

- **Send answer**: the dispute is cleared and the entry goes back on the client's list to approve. It bills on the next draft invoice. If its month's invoice was already issued, it becomes deferred work instead: it's billed from spare retainer hours on a later invoice, or with a buy-down. To change the hours first, edit the entry before you answer.
- **Write off**: the entry is never billed. Only someone who may approve time on the project can write it off, and only while no issued invoice charges it.

Expenses work the same way, and an owner or admin settles them.

## What Written off means

Written off time or an expense was disputed, and you settled the dispute by not billing it. The client sees it on their list, with your reply, for 30 days.

## What Invoiced means

Invoiced time is on an issued invoice and is locked.
