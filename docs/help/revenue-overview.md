Owners and admins see the money first on the dashboard. Everyone else sees only their own list of things to do.

## The key figures

Four tiles: **year to date**, **trailing 12 months**, the **next 12 months** and **open A/R**. Each has one line of context, such as how it compares with the same period last year.

**MRR** is recurring revenue normalised to a month. Annual items count one twelfth, quarterly items one third, semi-annual items one sixth, and one-time charges are left out.

**Contracted** revenue is what the agreements on file already commit clients to. An agreement stops contributing when it ends: no renewal is assumed.

## Invoiced or collected

The **Basis** toggle changes the tiles and the chart together.

- Invoiced counts issued invoices (never drafts or voided ones), in the month the service was for. It is the default.
- Collected counts payments received, net of refunds, on the day they arrived.

The forecast always stays on the invoiced basis, because a forecast of collections would need a model of how late clients pay. When you pick Collected the chart says so.

Revenue figures never include tax. Open A/R does, because it is a balance someone owes.

## The chart

Twelve months back, this month and twelve months ahead. Past months are stacked: recurring (retainer and recurring items), overage and other, and expenses. Future months show the contracted bar, with an optional hatched band for **Projected overage**.

- Hover a month to see its top five clients. Click a bar to open that month's invoices.
- **Show as table** lists every figure as text, with the same links. Screen readers always have the table.
- Turn expenses off to leave pass-through expense lines out of every figure.

A phone shows six months back and five ahead.

## At risk in the next 12 months

Every agreement that ends inside the window, with its end date and the monthly amount that drops off. Open the agreement to renew it or change its end date.
